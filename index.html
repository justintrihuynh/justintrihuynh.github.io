About me

How far can training take an athlete?

As a student-athlete at the University of Puget Sound, where I earned my bachelor's degree in exercise science, I was never the best athlete. Strength training was almost a mystery to me. As I got stronger, my performance improved, and I became fascinated with how training could help someone become a better athlete. That's what led me down the path of becoming a sports performance coach.

I've been fortunate to have a diverse range of experiences that have shaped how I approach coaching. At Cressey Sports Performance, I learned the value of assessments and understanding how an individual moves to help guide exercise selection. At Santa Clara University, I experienced coaching in a team setting and learned how to organize and manage a weight room. At Stanford University, I was introduced to applied sports science, which helped me appreciate objective data and the value of observing excellence every day. As a Graduate Assistant at Lamar University, where I earned my master's degree in kinesiology, I learned to be resourceful, work with limited resources, and embrace a blue-collar mindset.

Today, I lead strength and conditioning for women's basketball, women's tennis, and men's tennis at Boise State University, and support women's soccer through performance monitoring and Catapult GPS analysis.

Ultimately, I want my athletes to feel that I've helped unlock their athletic potential. Whether that's accomplishing something they didn't think was possible, becoming more confident in their abilities, or discovering another level of performance, I want them to leave knowing they're a better athlete because of the work we've done together. I also want them to take ownership of their development. I believe athletes should understand the purpose behind their training and, as they develop trust and experience, have the autonomy to make decisions within the process. To me, good coaching is about understanding the individual, continuing to learn, and helping each athlete become the best version of themselves.

Tools I've built

Men's tennis · Daily readiness

Wellness Check-in

A daily check-in athletes complete on their phones each morning, paired with a team view that coaches and the athletic trainer review before practice. It tracks readiness and session load in a sport where much of the training happens outside team practice.

  • Five 1–5 wellness scores, hours slept, sore body areas, and an optional note to staff.
  • Yesterday's sessions logged by type (practice, indi, lift, conditioning, match) with session RPE, post-session soreness, and minutes, so load is RPE × minutes.
  • Automatic flags: any score of 1–2, under 6 hours of sleep, a total 4+ below the athlete's own 3-week average, very sore after a session, or an RPE of 9–10.
  • Athletes see only their own answers. Staff see the team, trends, and history.

Force plates · Team dashboard

HawkinDash

A force-plate dashboard designed to organize jump testing into team summaries, athlete profiles, and weekly trends. The public demonstration uses simulated data to showcase the dashboard's workflow and functionality.

  • Team summary: total tests, athletes tested, average jump height, RSI, asymmetry, and testing frequency.
  • Athlete leaderboard with average and peak jump height, RSI, asymmetry, and trends.
  • Team trends across testing periods.
  • Filtering by team, test type, and date range.
Demo · deidentified, simulated data
Women's basketball · Performance testing

Performance Testing Dashboard

A dashboard that turns each testing day into team zones, individual profiles, and progress between sessions, so coaches can see each athlete's strengths and gaps at a glance.

  • Jumps (CMJ, approach, broad), speed (10-yard, flying 10, ¾ court), and change of direction (5-0-5 left and right).
  • Each result is placed in a zone from its team z-score (Elite, Above Avg, Below Avg, or Needs Work). The zones compare athletes within the testing group, not against external norms.
  • Speed view separates acceleration from top-end speed, and player profiles show percentiles with change since the last testing day.
  • Staff can enter results in the browser instead of editing a shared file.
